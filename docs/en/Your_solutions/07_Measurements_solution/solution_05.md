# 5. Percentage Calculation of Uncertainty

**Problem Statement:** An experimental measurement of time is recorded as $t = 5.45 \pm 0.22 \, \text{seconds}$. Our objective is to evaluate the precision of this measurement by calculating its **percentage uncertainty**.

---

### **I. Theoretical Definition**
Percentage uncertainty represents the ratio of the absolute uncertainty ($\Delta t$) to the measured value ($t$), scaled to a percentage. It serves as a direct indicator of experimental quality and precision.

* **Fundamental Governing Formula:**
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{\Delta t}{t} \right) \times 100\%$$

---

### **II. Step-by-Step Numerical Evaluation**
To find the final precision value, we process the extracted variables through our formula systematically across four explicit steps:

#### **Step 1: Identify Extracted Variables**
We look at our data and extract the fundamental parameters:
* Measured time ($t$) = $5.45 \, \text{seconds}$
* Absolute uncertainty ($\Delta t$) = $0.22 \, \text{seconds}$

#### **Step 2: Apply Variables to the Formula**
We substitute our two identified values directly into our primary percentage equation:
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{0.22}{5.45} \right) \times 100\%$$

#### **Step 3: Perform Algebraic Division**
We perform the division inside the parenthesis first to find the relative error as a decimal:
$$\frac{0.22}{5.45} \approx 0.040366$$

#### **Step 4: Multiply by One Hundred for Final Percentage**
To convert our decimal value into a standard percentage format, we multiply by $100$:
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = 0.040366 \times 100\% \approx 4.04\%$$

---

### **Conclusion**
The percentage uncertainty of the recorded time measurement is approximately **$4.04\%$**. This relatively small percentage value proves that our time measurement has a very high level of experimental precision and a low error rate, confirming a successful laboratory process.
