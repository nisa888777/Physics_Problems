# 7. Analysis of Energy and Charge in Parallel Capacitors

**Problem Statement:** Two capacitors, $C_1 = 4 \, \mu F$ and $C_2 = 6 \, \mu F$, are connected in parallel to a $V = 10 \, V$ DC source.

### **I. Calculation of Total Capacitance ($C_{tot}$)**
For capacitors connected in parallel, the total capacitance is the direct algebraic sum of individual capacitances because the effective surface area of the plates increases.
* **Formula:** $C_{tot} = C_1 + C_2$
* **Substitution:** $C_{tot} = 4 \, \mu F + 6 \, \mu F = 10 \, \mu F$
* **SI Unit Conversion:** $10 \times 10^{-6} \, F$

### **II. Determination of Total Stored Charge ($Q_{tot}$)**
Charge is defined by the relationship between capacitance and the potential difference (voltage) across it.
* **Fundamental Equation:** $Q = C \cdot V$
* **Substitution:** $Q_{tot} = 10 \times 10^{-6} \, F \cdot 10 \, V$
* **Result:** $Q_{tot} = 100 \, \mu C$ (or $10^{-4} \, C$)

### **III. Determination of Total Stored Energy ($U$ or $E$)**
The potential energy stored in the electric field of the capacitors is calculated using the following quadratic relationship:
* **Formula:** $U = \frac{1}{2} C_{tot} V^2$
* **Calculation:** $U = 0.5 \cdot (10 \times 10^{-6} \, F) \cdot (10 \, V)^2$
* **Simplification:** $U = 0.5 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 100 = 0.0005 \, J$
* **Result:** $U = 500 \, \mu J$

**Conclusion:** The parallel system stores $100 \, \mu C$ of charge and $500 \, \mu J$ of energy.
