## 1. Projectile Motion
**Problem:** A projectile is fired with $v_0 = 100 \text{ m/s}$ at an angle of $37^\circ$.

* **Step 1: Horizontal and Vertical Velocity Components**
  We use trigonometry to split the initial velocity:
  - $v_{0x} = v_0 \cdot \cos(37^\circ) = 100 \cdot 0.8 = 80 \text{ m/s}$ (Constant horizontal speed)
  - $v_{0y} = v_0 \cdot \sin(37^\circ) = 100 \cdot 0.6 = 60 \text{ m/s}$ (Initial vertical speed)

* **Step 2: Time of Flight ($T$)**
  The time it takes to go up and come back down is given by:
  $T = \frac{2 \cdot v_{0y}}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 60}{9.8} = \frac{120}{9.8} \approx 12.24 \text{ s}$

* **Step 3: Maximum Height ($H$)**
  The peak height reached by the projectile:
  $H = \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = \frac{60^2}{2 \cdot 9.8} = \frac{3600}{19.6} \approx 183.67 \text{ m}$

* **Step 4: Range ($R$)**
  The total horizontal distance covered:
  $R = v_{0x} \cdot T = 80 \cdot 12.24 = 979.2 \text{ m}$
