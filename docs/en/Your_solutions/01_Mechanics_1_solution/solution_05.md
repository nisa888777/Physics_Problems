## 5. Relative Velocity
**Problem:** A river flows east at $2 \text{ m/s}$. A boat travels at $5 \text{ m/s}$. To go directly north across a $200\text{m}$ wide river, find the direction (angle) and time.

* **Step 1: Determine the Heading Angle ($\theta$)**
  The boat must head west of north to cancel the river's current:
  $\sin(\theta) = \frac{v_{river}}{v_{boat}} = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4$
  $\theta = \arcsin(0.4) \approx 23.58^\circ$ (West of North)

* **Step 2: Calculate the Resultant Velocity ($v_y$)**
  The effective speed moving across the river (North) is:
  $v_y = \sqrt{v_{boat}^2 - v_{river}^2} = \sqrt{25 - 4} = \sqrt{21} \approx 4.58 \text{ m/s}$

* **Step 3: Calculate the Time to Cross**
  $t = \frac{\text{Width}}{v_y} = \frac{200}{4.58} \approx 43.67 \text{ s}$
