## 8. Circular Motion
**Problem:** Calculate the centripetal acceleration of a person standing on the Earth's equator ($R \approx 6378 \text{ km}$).

* **Step 1: Unit Conversion and Constants**
  - Radius ($R$): $6378 \text{ km} = 6.378 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$
  - Period ($T$): $24 \text{ hours} = 86,400 \text{ s}$ (Time for one full rotation)

* **Step 2: Calculate Tangential Velocity ($v$)**
  The speed at the equator is:
  $v = \frac{2\pi R}{T} = \frac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot 6,378,000}{86,400} \approx 463.83 \text{ m/s}$

* **Step 3: Calculate Centripetal Acceleration ($a_c$)**
  Using the formula $a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$:
  $a_c = \frac{(463.83)^2}{6,378,000} = \frac{215,138.27}{6,378,000} \approx 0.0337 \text{ m/s}^2$

* **Conclusion:** The centripetal acceleration at the equator is approximately **$0.0337 \text{ m/s}^2$**. This is much smaller than the gravitational acceleration ($9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).
