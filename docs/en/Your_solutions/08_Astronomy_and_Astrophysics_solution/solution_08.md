# 8. Interplanetary Travel Times to Mars

**Problem Statement:** How much time would it take to get from Earth to Mars when Mars is at its closest approach ($55 \, \text{million km}$):
* **a)** for a message sent at the speed of light?
* **b)** by a spacecraft traveling at a constant speed of $40,000 \, \text{km/h} = 11.11 \, \text{km/s}$?
* **c)** by "airplane" traveling at a constant speed of $900 \, \text{km/h}$, typical for intercontinental flights?

---

### **I. Theoretical Core Concept**
Every calculation in this problem relies on the fundamental physics relationship between distance, constant velocity, and elapsed time. 

* **Primary Governing Formula:**
$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} \implies t = \frac{x}{v}$$

* **Constant Parameter:**
  * Minimum Interplanetary Distance ($x$) = $55 \, \text{million km} = 55,000,000 \, \text{km} = 5.5 \times 10^7 \, \text{km}$

---

### **II. Case-by-Case Numerical Evaluation**

#### **Case a) Communication via Speed of Light**
We evaluate a radio or laser signal traveling through the vacuum of space at the constant speed of light ($v_c \approx 300,000 \, \text{km/s}$):
$$t = \frac{55,000,000 \, \text{km}}{300,000 \, \text{km/s}} \approx 183.33 \, \text{seconds}$$
To express this in a practical unit, we divide the seconds by $60$:
$$t = \frac{183.33}{60} \approx 3.06 \, \text{minutes}$$

#### **Case b) Travel via Modern Spacecraft**
We evaluate a deep-space probe cruising at a constant speed of $v = 40,000 \, \text{km/h}$ (which is equal to $11.11 \, \text{km/s}$):
$$t = \frac{55,000,000 \, \text{km}}{40,000 \, \text{km/h}} = 1375 \, \text{hours}$$
To convert these hours into days, we divide by the $24$-hour cycle of a standard day:
$$t = \frac{1375}{24} \approx 57.29 \, \text{days}$$

#### **Case c) Travel via Commercial Airplane**
We imagine a theoretical commercial jet flying through space at a typical cruise speed of $v = 900 \, \text{km/h}$:
$$t = \frac{55,000,000 \, \text{km}}{900 \, \text{km/h}} \approx 61111.11 \, \text{hours}$$
First, we divide by $24$ to discover the total days:
$$\text{Days} = \frac{61111.11}{24} \approx 2546.3 \, \text{days}$$
Next, we divide by $365$ to understand this massive duration in standard calendar years:
$$t = \frac{2546.3}{365} \approx 6.98 \, \text{years}$$

---

### **Summary of Final Results**
The calculated travel times clearly demonstrate the immense scale of our solar system:
* **Light-speed Message:** $\approx \mathbf{3.06 \, \text{minutes}}$
* **Spacecraft Rocket:** $\approx \mathbf{57.3 \, \text{days}}$
* **Commercial Jet:** $\approx \mathbf{6.98 \, \text{years}}$
