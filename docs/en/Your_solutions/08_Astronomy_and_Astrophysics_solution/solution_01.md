# 1. Rotational Velocity of the Earth

**Problem Statement:** Calculate the linear speed (in km/s) of a point on the Earth's equator due to its rotation. Earth's radius is given as $R \approx 6378 \, \text{km}$.

---

### **I. Theoretical Framework**
Linear speed ($v$) represents the total distance an object travels along a circular path divided by the time duration required to complete that path. For a spinning sphere like Earth, a point on the equator moves in a full circle every single day.

* **Governing Formulas:**
  1. Circumference of the equatorial circle (Total Distance, $x$):
  $$x = 2\pi R$$
  2. Linear Velocity formula ($v$):
  $$v = \frac{x}{T} = \frac{2\pi R}{T}$$

---

### **II. Step-by-Step Numerical Computation**
To determine the precise linear velocity in kilometers per second ($\text{km/s}$), we follow a structured three-step calculation:

#### **Step 1: Identify Extracted Parameters**
* Earth's Equatorial Radius ($R$) = $6378 \, \text{km}$
* Earth's Rotation Period ($T$) = $24 \, \text{hours}$

#### **Step 2: Standardize the Unit of Time**
Because the final answer must be in seconds, we convert the $24$-hour rotational period into standard seconds:
$$T = 24 \, \text{hours} \times 60 \, \text{minutes} \times 60 \, \text{seconds}$$
$$T = 86400 \, \text{seconds}$$

#### **Step 3: Apply Values to the Velocity Equation**
Now, we substitute our parameters into our primary linear velocity equation:
$$v = \frac{2 \times \pi \times 6378}{86400}$$
$$v \approx \frac{40074.155}{86400} \approx 0.4638 \, \text{km/s}$$

---

### **Conclusion**
The linear speed of an absolute point resting on the Earth's equator due to planetary rotation is approximately **$0.464 \, \text{km/s}$** (which is roughly $1670 \, \text{km/h}$).
